Kim — everything here is ready to use. Each post has the words, the photo to use, and a COPY button. Tap COPY, paste it into Facebook or Instagram, attach the photo, done. Nothing here has to be written by you.

Do it once a week, not every day

Twenty minutes on a Sunday covers the whole week. Here’s the routine:

  1. Open Meta Business Suite (free — business.facebook.com, or the Meta Business Suite app). It posts to Facebook and Instagram at the same time.
  2. Tap Create Post, then choose both Facebook and Instagram.
  3. Paste the words from below, attach the photo, and instead of Publish choose Schedule — pick the day and time listed.
  4. Repeat for that week’s three or four posts. Close the laptop. You’re done until next Sunday.

If Meta Business Suite feels like a lot, just post straight from the Instagram or Facebook app on the day. The words are the same.

The photos. Every photo below is already on your website, so you can save it straight to your phone: open the link, press and hold the picture, choose Save to Photos. Or use a fresh photo from the restaurant that night — a real plate that just went out always beats a stock shot.

Don’t forget Google

Your Google Business Profile lets you post too, and almost no restaurant bothers — which is exactly why it works. Google shows those posts right next to your listing when someone searches for you.

  1. Search TJ’s Italian Cafe on Google while signed in as the owner.
  2. In the panel that appears, tap Promote or Add update.
  3. Paste any of the posts above, add the photo, publish.

Once a week is plenty. The brunch post and the Early Evening post are the two worth putting there — those are what people search for.

Five things worth knowing

  • A real photo beats a perfect one. A plate leaving the kitchen on your phone will outperform a professional shot almost every time. People can tell.
  • Post before people are hungry, not during. Late morning for lunch and dinner; Friday afternoon for the weekend. Once they’re hungry they’ve already decided.
  • Answer every comment, even just a thank you. It tells Facebook and Instagram the post is worth showing to more people — and it’s good manners.
  • When something happens, post it. A full patio, a birthday table, a delivery of fish, TJ pulling a pizza. Those beat anything planned.
  • Ask happy customers for a Google review. One sentence mentioning what they ate is worth more than a week of posts for getting found.
